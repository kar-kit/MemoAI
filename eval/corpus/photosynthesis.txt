Biology 101 - Photosynthesis

[Slide 2]
Overview
Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
Overall equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light -> C6H12O6 + 6O2
Takes place in chloroplasts, mainly in leaf mesophyll cells

[Slide 3]
Chloroplast structure
Outer and inner membrane
Thylakoids: flattened membrane sacs, stacked into grana
Stroma: fluid surrounding the thylakoids
Chlorophyll a and b sit in the thylakoid membrane and absorb mostly red and blue light; green light is reflected, which is why leaves look green

[Slide 4]
Stage 1 - Light-dependent reactions
Location: thylakoid membrane
Photosystem II absorbs light and splits water (photolysis), releasing O2, protons and electrons
Electrons pass along the electron transport chain to Photosystem I
The proton gradient drives ATP synthase to make ATP (photophosphorylation)
Photosystem I re-energises electrons, which reduce NADP+ to NADPH
Outputs: ATP, NADPH, O2

[Slide 5]
Stage 2 - Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)
Location: stroma
Carbon fixation: the enzyme RuBisCO attaches CO2 to ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP)
The product splits into two molecules of glycerate 3-phosphate (GP)
Reduction: ATP and NADPH convert GP into triose phosphate (TP)
Regeneration: most TP is used to regenerate RuBP, using more ATP
Some TP leaves the cycle to build glucose and other organic molecules

[Slide 6]
Limiting factors
Light intensity, CO2 concentration and temperature
The rate is set by whichever factor is in shortest supply
High temperatures denature enzymes such as RuBisCO and reduce the rate

[Slide 7]
Key facts to remember
The oxygen released comes from water, not from CO2
RuBisCO is thought to be the most abundant protein on Earth
The Calvin cycle does not use light directly but depends on ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent stage
